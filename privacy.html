Privacy

Last updated 24 September 2026

MetaSelf is a personal Android application written for a single user. It is not published on any app store, has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no operator. There is no server belonging to this application, and nobody — including its author — can see what you record in it.

What it stores, and where

Everything you log lives on your phone: meals and their nutrition figures, weights, your profile, your goal, your settings. It is held in the application's own private storage, which no other app can read.

Your OpenAI API key, and your Open Food Facts password if you choose to save one, are stored encrypted against your phone's hardware keystore and are excluded from Android's automatic backup. They are never displayed again after being entered, and never written to any log or export.

What leaves your phone, and when

Five things, each only when you do something that requires it.

  1. A meal description, to OpenAI. When you ask for an estimate from words, the text you typed is sent using your own API key. Nothing else goes with it — not your weight, age, goal, history or any identifier. Repeating a meal, or typing the numbers yourself, sends nothing at all.
  2. One food's name and figures, to OpenAI, when you ask for a review. When you press Review the figures on a food, that one food is sent using your own API key: its name and brand, the figures it holds and where each came from, what one of it is called, and what one of it weighs. Nothing else goes with it — no other food, not what or when you ate, not your weight, age, goal or any identifier. Nothing is sent unless you press the button.
  3. A barcode number, to Open Food Facts. Only the number. The camera is used for nothing but reading barcodes; the picture is read on the phone and never stored or sent. A product you have scanned before is answered from your own phone and sends nothing.
  4. A product you choose to contribute, to Open Food Facts. Only if you have entered an account, and only when you press the button, one product at a time. What is sent is the barcode, name, brand, serving size and the figures on the label — plus your Open Food Facts username, because that database credits contributions. Nothing about you goes with it: not your weight, your history, or what or when you ate.
  5. A backup file, to your own Google Drive, if you turn that on. The application requests the drive.file scope, which grants access only to files the application itself creates. It cannot see, list, open or modify anything else in your Drive. The file is the same export you can save by hand, and your API key is deliberately not in it.

That is the complete list. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier, no tracking of any kind, and no other network destination.

Health and fitness data

If you allow it, the application reads steps, active calories and exercise sessions from Android's Health Connect, in order to work out whether you have moved more than usual on a given day.

This data is used on your phone, for that purpose, and is never transmitted anywhere. The application never writes to Health Connect. You can withdraw the permission at any time and it simply stops reading it.

Your data is yours

You can export everything to a single readable file at any time, and keep it wherever you like. You can have a copy written automatically into a folder of your choosing.

To delete everything, uninstall the application. Android removes its private storage with it. There is nothing held anywhere else to delete, because there is nowhere else.

Children

This application is not intended for, or directed at, children.
