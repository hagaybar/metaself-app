# A short fast says so — D109 (amends D32's fifth sentence)

> Written 2026-10-10. A time corrected on the day (D33) can move a morning input earlier and turn a
> completed fast into a short one. Nothing is miscounted then; the sentence today says about it is the
> trouble. **Every ratio, time and duration in an example is invented.**

## 1. What goes wrong

Under a ratio the eating stretch is the unit (D31): consecutive inputs belong to one stretch while
the gap between them is shorter than the fasting hours. A night's fast that falls short does not close
the stretch, so yesterday's eating and this morning's become one stretch. That is right as a record,
and the verdict that follows from it — broken, once a full fast completes — is right too.

What today then says is not. On a 14/10, with eating from 10:00 to 20:00 yesterday and the first input
today at 08:00, looked at 09:00, D32's fifth sentence says:

> Good morning. You've eaten for 22h 0m — next meal from 22:00 if you're keeping your fast.

Both halves mislead.

- **"Eaten for 22h"** counts a twelve-hour night as eating. What happened is that the fast came up two
  hours short.
- **"Next meal from 22:00"** asks for fourteen more hours from a breakfast. The stretch is already
  over the hours and its verdict is already settled — no amount of fasting now saves it — so the
  advice costs a day's eating and buys nothing. Every later input moves it further: by mid-morning
  the time it offers is after midnight.

## 2. The rule

**A short fast is a gap inside a stretch that is longer than the eating hours.** It is necessarily
shorter than the fasting hours, or it would have closed the stretch.

The threshold is the ratio's own, not a new number. No gap longer than the eating hours can sit inside
a stretch that keeps the ratio, because the span would already exceed them; such a gap is a fast that
was attempted, not eating. A gap of exactly the eating hours can sit in a kept stretch (two inputs, the
eating hours apart) and so is not one. A 12/12 has no short fasts at all: any gap over twelve hours
reaches the fast and closes the stretch.

**When the latest stretch is OPEN and holds a short fast, today's sentence says so, and gives its time
from the first input after the LATEST short fast** — call it **F′** — rather than from the stretch's
first input. With **L** the last input, the closing time is **E′ = F′ + the eating hours**, and the
next meal, if the fast is being kept, is from **X = L + the fasting hours**, unchanged.

| # | Situation | What today says |
|---|---|---|
| 5a | A short fast, before E′ | "Your last fast ended at 08:00 after 12h 0m; your ratio asks for 14 hours. If you're keeping your window from there, last meal by 18:00." |
| 5b | A short fast, past E′, eating since F′ inside the hours | "… ; your ratio asks for 14 hours. If you're keeping your fast, next meal from 08:00 tomorrow." |
| 5c | A short fast, past E′, eating since F′ over the hours | "… ; your ratio asks for 14 hours. You've eaten for 11h 0m since then — next meal from 09:00 tomorrow if you're keeping your fast." |

- **The first half is a fact, the second a condition**, as D32 requires of every sentence about the
  future. Neither reproaches (D14); neither counts down (D15, D27).
- **The fast's length rounds DOWN to the minute**, where a span rounds up: both are the direction that
  cannot flatter.
- **Its end is said like row 2's moment**: "at 08:00", "yesterday at 08:00", or "on 18 Sep at 08:00".
- **The latest short fast, not the longest.** After two short nights the advice belongs to this
  morning, not yesterday's.
- **"Good morning." still opens it before noon.**
- **The rows D32 already had are unchanged for a stretch with no short fast** — including row 5,
  which remains the sentence for a ratio whose fast is never reached in a stretch with no long gaps,
  such as a day of eating every few hours. And row 2 is unchanged: once the fast completes, a short
  fast inside the stretch is history, and the fast that ended it is what is said.

## 3. What does not change

- **The stretch rule, every verdict, and the tally.** The stretch still runs from yesterday's first
  input and will be judged broken once a full fast completes. This changes what today says, not what
  is counted.
- **A past day's sentences** — the span, the still-open line, the one-input line.
- **No stored data.** The short fast is read off times already on the record: no schema, no migration.

Considered and not done: **splitting a stretch at a short fast.** It would give today a stretch of its
own, but it would make a short fast count as a completed one, so a night two hours short would leave
both days kept. That flatters, and the tally would stop meaning what it says.

## 4. Left, deliberately

- **No separate notice when a corrected time changes a past verdict.** A correction that shortens a
  fast is exactly what produces a short fast, and today's sentence now names it with its length and the
  ratio beside it, which is the explanation such a notice would have given. The tally beside it moves
  without comment, as it does whenever the record changes.
- A short fast inside a stretch that has already closed is not spoken about on its past day; that
  day's span line says the stretch ran over, as before.
