# A meal can be worked out in conversation — D58

> Decided 2026-09-25 by the owner. Describing a meal used to be one question and one answer: the
> model guessed whatever the words left open — how big the box, which cheese, how much oil — and
> the guess looked like any other number. From D58 the model may first ask a few short questions,
> one at a time, each chosen for what most changes the calories, and then works the meal out the way
> a nutritionist reconstructs a plate, saying for every item what it assumed and why.
>
> Every meal, place, answer and figure below is invented to illustrate the rule beside it.

---

## Why there is a decision here at all

- **The biggest errors in a described meal are the things nobody says.** The oil in the pan, the
  dressing, how full the container was, whether the cheese was a sprinkle or a layer. The model
  cannot see the plate, and the owner cannot be expected to know which of these matter. The model
  can: it knows which unknowns move the total most, and it can ask about those.
- **Today's answer to an unclear meal is *Tell it more*** — one free sentence after the fact, with no
  hint of what was missing. A question with ready-made answers costs one tap and says exactly what
  the model needed.
- **Not every meal needs it.** An apple, a named packaged bar, a coffee: the answer is the same with
  or without questions, and asking would be a toll. So the model decides, and says how many
  questions it wants before the first one is asked.

---

## D58 — A described meal may be worked out in a short conversation; only the result is kept

### 1. Where it starts

Two ways in, one flow:

- **Add something → Describe a meal** (and *Describe "…" instead* from its search), as today. The
  describe screen and its box are unchanged; *Work it out* now starts the flow below.
- **My meals → Describe a meal** — a new button on My meals' tab, beside *Build a meal*, opening the
  same screen. The route carries where it came from; that changes nothing in the flow, only where
  the owner lands afterwards (§5.3).

### 2. The flow

1. **He describes the meal in his own words**: what it was, where from (the kind of place), what he
   knows went into it, how it was made. The box is the one there is today.
2. **The first request** sends the description and asks the model to decide whether questions are
   needed (§3.1).
   - **None needed** — "an apple", "a 330 ml can of cola" — the same request carries the estimate,
     and the proposal screen opens exactly as today. **One request, as today.**
   - **Questions needed** — the same request carries the first question and how many the model
     plans. The screen says:

     > *I'd like to ask up to 3 questions to get this right.*
     > **[OK]** **[Use your best guess]**

     *OK* shows the first question, already in hand — no request. *Use your best guess* goes
     straight to the final analysis (§2.4) with the description alone.
3. **Questions, one at a time, adaptive.** Each question after the first is generated by its own
   request, after the previous answer, so each can use everything said so far. Each shows:
   - a heading *Question 2 of up to 3*;
   - the question, and **its ready-made answers** as buttons — a tap answers and moves on;
   - **Other: …** — a text box and *Answer*, on every question, for anything the buttons miss;
   - **That's enough, go ahead** — straight to the final analysis with what has been said so far;
   - **Back** — to the previous question (§2.5).

   **At most five questions**, and never more than the number offered (§7 may offer fewer). After the
   last offered question is answered, the final analysis follows without asking the model whether
   it wants another. Before that, a request that answers *no more questions* goes to the final
   analysis too.
4. **The final analysis** — one request, with more thinking than the everyday describe (§8) and its
   own instructions (§3.2) — returns the meal as items, and the proposal screen opens on them (§5).
5. **Back.** The question's **Back** button, the screen's back arrow and the phone's back gesture all
   step back one stage: a question → the one before it → the offer → the description, with his words
   in the box. Only from the description does back leave the screen, as today.
   - **Going back costs nothing.** The earlier question is shown from memory, with his answer
     selected (or his *Other* words in the box).
   - **Answering it the same way** moves forward to the question that followed it, again from memory,
     with its answer selected — no request.
   - **Answering it differently discards every later question and answer**, and the next question is
     asked again: **one request**, counted, because the questions after it were chosen in the light
     of the old answer.
   - Back from the offer drops the first request's answer; *Work it out* again is a new first request.

   *Why back steps rather than leaves:* a conversation is a sequence, and a back gesture that threw
   away three answers (and the requests they cost) would be the harshest reading of an accidental
   swipe. The cost accepted is that leaving mid-conversation takes a few presses.

### 3. What is asked

Every request is built by the one request builder (`ChatRequest`) from the model's profile (D57).
What each says is below; the wording in the code may be tightened, the substance may not.

The messages, in order, for every request of a conversation:

| Role | Content |
|---|---|
| `system` | The instructions for this kind of request (§3.1 or §3.2). |
| `user` | The description, as typed. |
| `assistant`, `user` | For each question answered so far: the question as the model asked it, then his answer — the text of the button he tapped, or his *Other* words. A tapped *Not sure* is sent as its text. |
| `system` | For a question step only: *"Questions asked so far: 2. You may ask at most 1 more."* — the app's own sentence, with the app's own numbers. |

Nothing else is sent: no date, no time, no body, no history, no foods of his (§10).

#### 3.1 Deciding, and choosing the next question

The first request and every question step share these instructions. The first request adds the
everyday estimate's rules (today's `EstimatePrompt` instructions, word for word) for the case where
no question is needed.

> You help work out the calories of one meal from the eater's description. Before estimating, you
> may ask a few short questions, one at a time. Decide whether you need to.
>
> - **Ask only when an answer could change the meal's total by roughly a tenth or more.** A single
>   plain food (a fruit, a boiled egg), a packaged product with its size, a standard drink: no
>   questions. Then set `needs_questions` to false.
> - **Ask about the biggest calorie uncertainty first**, in this order unless the meal says
>   otherwise:
>   1. **Added fats** — cooking oil, butter, ghee, dressing, mayonnaise, a drizzle at the end: whether
>      there was any, and how much.
>   2. **Portion size** — the container or plate and how full it was, how many scoops, pieces or
>      servings.
>   3. **The type of an energy-dense part** — which cheese, a cream or a tomato sauce, which
>      dressing, whole or skimmed milk, sugar in a drink.
>   4. **Cooking method** — fried, grilled, baked, steamed; breaded or not.
>   5. **Unmentioned extras that usually come with this dish** — bread on the side, a topping, a dip.
> - **Never ask what the description or an earlier answer already says**, directly or by clear
>   implication. Never ask about anything that does not change the calories.
> - **Ask what the eater could see or remember, never a measurement.** Not "how many grams", but the
>   size of the box against a hand, how many spoonfuls, whether the pasta was glossy or dry.
> - **Ask about the meal only.** The kind of place it came from is a fair question (home-cooked, a
>   restaurant, a takeaway counter, a bakery, packaged); its name, where it is, when he ate, who with,
>   and anything about the eater are never asked.
> - **One question at a time, one thing per question**, at most about twenty words, in the
>   description's language.
> - **Give 2 to 5 ready-made answers**: concrete, mutually exclusive, each short enough for a button,
>   ordered from least to most calories where that makes sense, and always ending with an answer
>   meaning "Not sure". The app adds a free-text answer itself; do not offer one.
> - `total_planned` is how many questions you expect to need in all, this one included — honestly,
>   and never more than you are allowed. Fewer is better: stop asking when the remaining unknowns
>   would change the total by little.

For a question step, `needs_questions` false means *no more questions*: the step carries nothing
else, and the final analysis follows.

#### 3.2 The final analysis

> You are a nutritionist working out the calories of one meal the eater described, and the
> answers they gave to your questions. Reconstruct the plate as it was served before you estimate.
>
> - **Start from where it came from.** How that kind of place makes and portions this dish: the
>   standard serving utensils (ladle, scoop, tongs, spoon), the containers and plate sizes, and the
>   usual portion. A home-cooked dish is portioned as a home cook would; a packaged one by its
>   package.
> - **Account for the cooking.** Oil absorbed in frying, butter or oil in a pan, sugar in a glaze,
>   the fat in a cream sauce. What the method adds belongs to the item it is cooked into.
> - **Look for the hidden calories** — oil, butter, dressing, sauce, spread, sugar — that the
>   description does not mention but that this dish from this kind of place normally has. Include
>   each one you believe was there. **An added fat or sauce the eater could see or leave (a dressing,
>   a drizzle, butter on bread, a dip) is its own item**, so it can be corrected; a fat cooked into a
>   component is part of that component's figures and is said in its detail.
> - **Resolve every ambiguity** — which cheese, which sauce, which bread — with the most likely
>   choice for this dish and this kind of place, and say which you chose.
> - **The answers override your defaults.** An answer "Not sure" means: use this kind of place's
>   usual practice, and lower the confidence for that item.
> - **Before the items, write the plate in `plate`**: a few short lines going through the source,
>   the portions, the cooking and the hidden calories. This is your working; it is not shown with
>   the items.
> - **Give each amount in the unit that fits how it was served.** For a loose, scooped, ladled or
>   plated component — rice, pasta, a salad's leaves, a stew, grated cheese, a sauce — give the
>   weight you worked out, in grams, with figures per 100 g, whenever your reconstruction supports
>   it (the utensil, the container, the place's usual portion). For a counted piece — a roll, an egg,
>   a slice, a bar, a drink — give the count in its own unit with figures per one piece. An amount
>   the eater stated is used exactly as stated.
> - **For every item, the detail is one line saying the amount's assumption and why** — for example
>   "two ladles into a standard takeaway box; thick cream sauce from the answer". At most about
>   fifteen words. **No grams in the detail**: the amount already says them.

Followed by the everyday rules, unchanged in force: every item with an amount greater than zero and
a unit (D34); a drink is one item in its usual serving (D35); the plain name, the unit as stated or
the natural piece, *g* and *ml* written exactly, figures per 100 or per one piece, never a range,
confidence LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH, the description's language (D53 §2); and at most one note, about the
biggest remaining uncertainty.

**Amended 2026-09-25, the owner: the final analysis may give an amount in grams it worked out.**
(Open question 1, answered.) In a conversation's final analysis only, a loose, scooped or plated
component may come back as *Quinoa, 70 g*, the reason reading *a serving spoon at such a counter;
two spoonfuls* (invented). How this sits with the earlier decisions:

- **D53 §2** forbade the model to invent a weight for an unstated amount, because an invented weight
  looks like a measurement. For the final analysis that rule is **lifted for the amount only**: the
  weight is worked out from the reconstruction the owner asked for — the place, its utensils, his
  answers — and it is the most checkable thing the model can say. Everywhere else D53 §2 stands
  unchanged: the everyday describe, and the first request's estimate when no question is needed,
  still give an unstated amount as one natural piece. **The detail still carries no invented grams**
  beyond restating the amount; its words say why, the amount says how much.
- **D4** is what keeps it honest: the row is `AI_ESTIMATE` with the model's confidence, labelled as
  an estimate on the proposal screen and on the record, like every figure the model gives. A weight
  worked out is never presented as a weight measured. Typing over the amount changes the total and
  nothing about the worth or the source (D53 §1, §3), as for any row.
- **D34** is unchanged: every item still needs an amount greater than zero and a unit, and the
  second ask still names the items without one. Grams are simply one more unit the answer may give;
  the reader's rules on a per-100 worth (grams or millilitres only, D42's ceilings, the row's
  ceiling) apply as they do to a weight the eater stated.
- **D35** is unchanged: a drink is still one item counted in its serving, not in millilitres
  worked out.

### 4. What comes back

Both schemas are strict (`json_schema`, `strict: true`); under D57's `json_object` fallback they are
written into the instructions. **Every reply is read field by field on the phone either way.**

#### 4.1 A question step (the first request, and every later step)

The question, for both:

```json
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["text", "options"],
  "properties": {
    "text":    { "type": "string" },
    "options": { "type": "array", "items": { "type": "string" } }
  }
}
```

A later step:

```json
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["needs_questions", "total_planned", "question"],
  "properties": {
    "needs_questions": { "type": "boolean" },
    "total_planned":   { "type": "integer" },
    "question":        QUESTION
  }
}
```

The first request is the same object with two more required properties, `items` — today's item
schema, unchanged (`EstimatePrompt`'s: `name`, `detail`, `amount`, `unit`, `figures_per`, `kcal`,
`protein_g`, `carbs_g`, `fat_g`, `confidence`) — and `note`, a string. With questions, `items` is
an empty array.

**The question is always an object, never null** (*amended after review*). When there is no question
its `text` and `options` are empty. A nullable object (`anyOf` with `null`) was the first shape
drawn; it was dropped because the first request now replaces the everyday describe for every meal,
a model that refused that schema would refuse every description, and D57 deliberately learns nothing
from a refusal of the app's own schema.

**Read as:**

| Reply | Read as |
|---|---|
| `needs_questions` false, first request | An estimate: `items` and `note` read by today's reader (`EstimateResponse`) with all its rules — an item dropped and named, D34's second ask, *unreadable* when nothing is left. `question` is ignored. |
| `needs_questions` false, later step | No more questions: the final analysis follows. |
| `needs_questions` true | A question: `text` trimmed and not blank; `options` trimmed, blanks and repeats (ignoring case) removed, the first five kept. **If none of them means *Not sure*, the phone adds *Not sure* itself** — never unreadable for that. `total_planned` below 1 is 1, above 5 is 5. `items` is ignored. |
| `needs_questions` missing, `items` present (the `json_object` fallback of D57, loosely followed) | Read as `needs_questions` false: an estimate on the first request. |
| `needs_questions` false alone on a later step | No more questions. |
| Anything else — no question text or not one usable answer while `needs_questions` is true, not JSON | **An unreadable step** (§9). |

**No `allow_other` field.** The owner's design gives every question an *Other* answer, so a flag
that let the model withhold it could only break that. The phone adds *Other* itself.

#### 4.2 The final analysis

```json
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["plate", "items", "note"],
  "properties": {
    "plate": { "type": "string" },
    "items": { "type": "array", "items": ITEM },
    "note":  { "type": "string" }
  }
}
```

`ITEM` is today's item, **exactly** — no new field. `plate` comes first so the model writes its
working before its figures; the phone reads nothing from it. The reply is read by today's reader
unchanged (it already ignores fields it does not use), so every rule of D34, D35, D42 and D53 §2
applies as it does to any described meal.

**The per-item reason is the existing `detail`**, not a parallel field. `detail` is already "the
assumption behind the numbers" (D5): it is shown under the item's name on the proposal screen and
kept in the logged row's portion words — *350 g (two ladles into a standard takeaway box; cream
sauce assumed from the answer)*. That is where a reason belongs, and it survives as the only trace of the
conversation (§6). The detail says the assumption in words and carries no grams of its own; an
amount in grams is the amount's, as §3.2 amended says.

### 5. The result, and the end choice

#### 5.1 The proposal screen, as it is

The result opens on today's proposal screen, unchanged in what it does: every item's amount, worth
and removal are his to correct; his own foods are matched on the phone (D53 §4, §5); every row says
where its figures came from. Two additions:

- **After a conversation, *Show the model's answer* is always offered** (not only when an item was
  dropped), since the plate's working is worth reading there. Shown only, as today: never stored,
  never logged.
- **After a conversation, *Anything it got wrong?* → *Ask again* re-runs the final analysis** with
  the same answers and his sentence added as *More detail: …* — one request (plus D34's second ask
  if needed). Without a conversation (an apple), it is today's *Ask again*, unchanged.

#### 5.2 Log it / Keep as a meal / Both

Three buttons at the foot, for both ways in:

| Button | Today | D58 |
|---|---|---|
| **Log it** | *Save this meal* | Renamed. Same act: the rows go on the day being looked at. |
| **Keep as a meal** | — | **New.** Names a meal and saves it to My meals, **logging nothing**. |
| **Log it and keep as a meal** | *Save, and keep these as a meal* | Renamed. Same act (D46): the rows are logged, then named as a meal on the day's own sheet. |

The filled button is the one the entry point is for — *Log it* from Add something, *Keep as a meal*
from My meals; the other two are outlined. Order is the same both ways.

**Keep as a meal**, the one new act:

- It opens the **same naming sheet** the day uses (`MealNamingSheet`), showing the rows as they would
  be logged, and nothing is written until he confirms a name. Not now closes the sheet and leaves
  the answer on screen.
- On a name: each row's food is found or made, and taught the row's worth, exactly as logging
  would do it (`LoggedFoods.attach`, D53 §3); the parts are the rows' amounts, converted by the same
  rules the day uses to make a meal from rows (`MealFromDay`), with the same refusals. **Foods, the
  meal and its parts are written in one transaction** — a refused name or a part that cannot join
  leaves no new food behind — and a refusal is said in the sheet, as on the day.
- **Nothing is logged.** The meal's figures are the parts' foods' figures from now on, as for any
  meal he builds.

**A meal of one is a food** (D46): with one row, *Keep as a meal* and *Log it and keep as a meal* are
not drawn, as today's offer is not; the line *One item is a food, not a meal — logging it keeps it in
your foods.* stands in their place.

#### 5.3 Where he lands

**Where what was stored is**, whichever way he came in: after *Log it* or *Log it and keep as a
meal*, the day (with the naming sheet for the latter, as today); after *Keep as a meal*, My meals,
where the new meal is on the list.

### 6. What is kept

- **Nothing is stored until the end choice.** The description, the questions, the answers and the
  answer as it came live in the screen's memory (they survive rotating the phone, not the app being
  closed by the system). Leaving at any point before the choice stores nothing.
- **The conversation itself is never kept** — not on the record, not in the backup, not in the
  problem log. Only the result is: rows and/or a meal, like any described meal.
- **Each item's detail carries its reason** into the logged row's portion words (§4.2). A meal kept
  without logging keeps foods and amounts only — a saved meal's part has no words of its own — so
  its reasons end with the screen.
- **No database change.** Nothing new is stored anywhere except one optional field in D57's
  remembered profiles (§8), which live in the settings store.

### 7. Requests, and the day's ceiling

**Every request that reaches the provider counts against the day's ceiling (D57 §4), and the
conversation is shaped around that.**

| Case | Requests |
|---|---|
| No questions needed (an apple) | **1**, as today |
| Questions offered, *Use your best guess* | 2 — the first request, the final analysis |
| N questions answered | **N + 1** — the first request (carrying question 1), one step for each of questions 2 to N, the final analysis |
| Stopped early, by *That's enough* while question k is shown | k + 1 |
| Stopped early, by the model answering *no more questions* after question k | k + 2 — that step, and the final analysis |
| **Most, as designed** | **6** — five questions |

On top of those, each only when it happens: D34's second ask when the final analysis leaves an
amount out (+1, so **7 at the very most** for a conversation that goes straight through); each
changed answer after *Back* (+1); each *Try again* (+1); *Ask again* on the result (+1); and D57's
learning retries, at most three per request and only while a model is new to the app.

**One request is always kept in reserve for the result.** The phone reads the day's remaining
allowance before offering and before each step:

- **The offer** is *up to N* where N is the smallest of the model's `total_planned`, 5, and the
  allowance remaining after the first request (question 1 is already in hand, so N questions need
  N − 1 steps and the final analysis: N requests). With none remaining, no offer is made: the
  ceiling's sentence is shown and his words are kept (D8).
- **Before a question step**, when only one request remains, no step is sent: the screen says
  *Today's allowance has room for the result only, so it's being worked out with what you've said so
  far.* and the final analysis follows.
- **Before the final analysis**, when none remains — possible after *Try again* or *Back* — the
  ceiling's sentence is shown, his description and answers stay on screen, and *Type the numbers
  myself* is offered with his words carried, as today. Nothing is stored.

### 8. More thinking for the final analysis, within D57

D57 remembers, per model name, **what the model accepts**: temperature or none, a `reasoning_effort`
value or none, and the reply format. That profile is what every everyday request is sent with, and
D58 does not change it.

**The final analysis asks for `high` effort where the model takes an effort at all:**

1. It starts from the same profile an everyday request would use — the remembered one, or D57 §2's
   guess. Because the first request of a conversation always comes first, that profile has in
   practice just worked.
2. **If that profile sends a `reasoning_effort` (any value), the final analysis sends `high`
   instead** — or the model's remembered *deep* level (below), when there is one. Temperature and
   format are sent as the profile says.
3. **If it sends none** — a model sent `temperature: 0`, like today's default — **it is sent exactly
   as the profile says.** Adding an effort such a model has refused would spend a request to learn
   what is already known. For such a model the deeper analysis comes from the instructions and the
   `plate` working alone.
4. **A refusal of `high` is learned by D57 §3's table, aimed at `high` instead of `low`** (the
   amendment below): with a list of accepted values, the highest accepted value at or below `high`;
   without a list, the next value down. Within D57's bounds — three retries, none repeated, none past
   the ceiling, each counted.
5. **What worked is remembered as the model's deep level**, a separate optional field in the same
   remembered entry — `{"temperature":false,"reasoning_effort":"low","format":"strict",
   "deep_reasoning_effort":"medium"}` (invented). **The everyday profile is never changed by a
   final analysis**: an everyday request keeps its own effort, and an everyday write keeps the deep
   field as it found it. Anything else a final analysis learns (a temperature or format refusal) is
   not remembered; the next everyday request learns it, as D57 already does. An entry without the
   field has no deep level remembered, and the next final analysis starts from `high`.
6. **A longer wait is allowed for this one request**: its call timeout is 120 seconds, not the 45
   every other request has, because a high effort can take longer and a timeout that fires first
   costs a paid request and gives nothing.

### 9. When something fails

Every failure is said in today's sentences (`ProposalWording.failure`) — no key, the ceiling, no
network, the provider's refusal, an unreadable reply — and none stores anything.

| Where | What he sees |
|---|---|
| **The first request** | Exactly as today's describe: the sentence, his words in the box, *Add a key* when that is the cause, *Show the model's answer* for an unreadable reply, *Type the numbers myself*. |
| **A question step** (unreachable, refused, unreadable) | The sentence, and two buttons: **Try again** (the same step, one request) and **Use your best guess with what you've said so far** (the final analysis). An unreadable step offers *Show the model's answer*. Back still works. |
| **The ceiling reached mid-conversation** | §7: straight to the final analysis while one request remains; otherwise the ceiling's sentence, his words and answers kept, *Type the numbers myself*. |
| **The final analysis** | The sentence, the conversation kept in memory, and **Try again** (the final analysis again — not the questions) or *Type the numbers myself*. An item it drops is named as today. |

**The problem log** records each failure by kind — *conversation step unreadable*, *final analysis
refused*, and so on, with the provider's words for a refusal as today — and **never** the
description, a question, an option or an answer: a question quotes the meal.

### 10. Privacy

**New words leave the phone: his answers.** Each is either the text of a button he tapped — words
the model itself proposed — or words he typed. With them go the model's own questions, sent back to
it. Nothing about him goes: not his body, his goal, his history, the date or time, his foods (own-food
matching stays on the phone, after the answer, D53 §4), or any identifier. Only the owner's key is
used, as before.

**D16, amended.** *Exactly the meal description leaves the phone to a third party* now reads:
*exactly the meal description — and, when the app asks about the meal, his answers and the questions
they answer — leaves the phone to the AI provider.* D54's promise for a review and D57 §7 are
unchanged.

**The privacy page** (`privacy.html`), first item of *What leaves your phone*, becomes:

> **A meal description, and your answers about it, to OpenAI.** When you ask for an estimate from
> words, the text you typed is sent using your own API key. If the app asks you questions about the
> meal first, each answer — the one you tapped or the words you typed — is sent too, with the
> questions it answers. Nothing else goes with it — not your weight, age, goal, history or any
> identifier. Repeating a meal, or typing the numbers yourself, sends nothing at all.

*Last updated* moves to the release date. *That is the complete list* stays true: still five things.

### 11. Every figure is an estimate (D4)

Every item a conversation proposes is `AI_ESTIMATE` with the model's confidence, exactly as a
described item is, whatever was answered: an answer narrows the guess, it does not measure anything.
The rest of D53 §3 applies unchanged — a row taking his own food's figures carries that food's
source; a figure he types makes the row `TYPED`.

### 12. Settled in review, 2026-09-25

A review of this design, before any of it was built, settled the following. Each overrides anything
above that reads otherwise.

**12.1 One conversation, one live request.** Every request carries the conversation's generation;
a reply to a request that has since been superseded — by Back, by leaving, by a second tap — is
dropped unread. **Back while waiting cancels the request in flight** and steps back; the request
still counts if it was sent (12.6). While a request is in flight the answer buttons, *Other*,
*That's enough* and *OK* / *Use your best guess* are disabled, so nothing is sent twice.

**12.2 Back and the answers, exactly.**

- The top bar's back arrow steps back exactly as the phone's back gesture and the question's Back do.
- *That's enough, go ahead* after going back uses the answers up to **and including** the question
  shown, when it has a remembered answer; later answers are dropped.
- *Use your best guess* from the offer uses no answers, even ones remembered from before going back.
- An answer is *the same* as the remembered one when the two are equal trimmed and ignoring case —
  a tapped button or *Other* words alike.
- The heading's *of up to N* is the latest `total_planned`, never above the number offered and never
  below the question's own number.

**12.3 A refusal never ends the conversation.** Anything the screen's safety net catches (the
`guarded` refusal) is said on the stage he is on, which is kept; it does not reset him to an empty
description.

**12.4 The allowance's last request.** When exactly one request remains before the final analysis,
it is sent as an everyday request — the everyday profile, no `high` effort — since a refusal of
`high` could not be answered by a retry anyway. D34's second ask is **not** part of the reserve: when
the final analysis leaves an amount out and nothing remains, D34's refusal is shown, as today.

**12.5 What D57 remembers, precisely.**

- **The everyday profile is remembered after every readable step**: the first request, a question,
  and a *no more questions* reply — any answer the conversation's reader could read.
- **A final analysis never writes the everyday profile.** It writes only the deep level.
- **The deep level is merged in the same single edit** as any everyday write, which never drops it.
  Written for a model with no entry, it creates a valid entry whose everyday part is the first guess.
- The deep level has three states: **absent** (start from `high`), **a value** (send it), and
  **explicitly none** — a final analysis on that model worked only with no `reasoning_effort` at all,
  so none is sent (stored as JSON `null`).

**12.6 Timeouts and counting** (fixed for every request, before D58 was built). Every request's
connect, write, read and call timeouts are set explicitly — 15 s to connect, 60 s for the rest — and
the final analysis has 120 s for write, read and call. OkHttp's ten-second read timeout had stayed
in force beneath the old 45-second call timeout. **A request written to the connection counts
against the ceiling even if its answer never arrives** (it may have been answered and billed); one
that never connected does not.

**12.7 Keep as a meal is all or nothing.** Finding or making the foods, teaching them the rows'
worth, making the meal and its parts run in **one** transaction. On this path a storage failure while
finding or teaching a food is not swallowed (logging swallows it, by design, so a row is still
logged): it ends the transaction. A refusal — the name taken, a part that cannot join — also rolls
everything back, including facts re-taught to a food he already had, and is said afterwards. The
refusal's words never say anything *was logged*, since nothing was; beside it, **Log it instead**.

**12.8 Log it from My meals logs on today**, explicitly, whatever day the day screen was last
showing, and he lands on today with the day's usual line about what was logged. From Add something,
logging is onto the day being looked at, as today. Where he lands after each choice is one pure rule
(§5.3), tested on its own.

**12.9 How an estimate reads on the day (D4).** A gram amount worked out by the final analysis is
stored as `AI_ESTIMATE` with its confidence, like every model figure. **The day's list shows no
source under any row** — a standing decision recorded in `DayTotalsWording.origin`: the list is for
seeing what was eaten, and the source is shown where he decides (the proposal screen's *Estimated —
… confidence*), kept on the record, in the row's editor and in every export. D58 does not change
that; whether the day should mark estimated amounts is a separate decision.

## Costs accepted

- **More requests per described meal when the model asks**: up to six as designed, seven with D34's
  second ask, all counted against the day's ceiling and paid for with his key. A meal that needs no
  questions costs one, as today.
- **Slower**: each question is a round trip, and the final analysis at high effort can take much
  longer than an everyday answer.
- **A bigger first request**: it carries both the question rules and the estimate rules, so every
  described meal sends longer instructions than today, and every answer can be a question instead of
  an estimate. The model may ask when it need not; *Use your best guess* is one tap.
- **The questions are the model's.** Two models may ask different things about the same meal, and a
  model may ask a question worse than the owner would have. The ready-made answers are its proposals;
  *Other* is his way past them.
- **The conversation is lost if the system closes the app mid-way**, because nothing is stored before
  the end choice.
- **A meal kept without logging does not keep the reasons**, since a meal's part has no words.
- **For a model that takes no thinking setting, "more thinking" is the instructions and the written
  working only.**

## What does not change

- Typing the numbers, repeating a meal or food, scanning a barcode, a food's review, Build a meal:
  untouched, and still send nothing new.
- The proposal screen's rows: amounts, worth, removal, own-food matching, *From your foods*, the
  source lines, *Show the model's answer*.
- D34, D35, D42 and D53 §2–§5 in force; D4's labelling; D6's *nothing saved until he accepts*.
- D57 for every request: the guess, the learning, the bounds, the counting, what is remembered for
  everyday requests, and Settings' *Test it*.
- The day's ceiling, its default and how it counts; the backup and its contents; the database.

## Worked example — invented

*Everything in this example is invented: the meal, the answers and every figure.*

He types: *"Pasta with a creamy mushroom sauce from a takeaway counter, grated cheese on top, and a
small bread roll."*

**Request 1** — the first request. The model judges the sauce, the portion and the cheese open, and
replies `needs_questions: true`, `total_planned: 3`, with question 1. With plenty of allowance left,
the screen offers: *I'd like to ask up to 3 questions to get this right.* He taps **OK**.

> **Question 1 of up to 3** — How big was the pasta container?
> [Small box, fits in one hand] [Standard takeaway box] [Large box, or two portions] [Not sure]

He taps *Standard takeaway box*. **Request 2** — a step, with that answer — asks:

> **Question 2 of up to 3** — What was the sauce like?
> [Thin, mostly at the bottom] [Coating the pasta] [Thick and creamy, pooling] [Oily and glossy] [Not sure]

He taps *Thick and creamy, pooling*. **Request 3** asks:

> **Question 3 of up to 3** — How much grated cheese was on top?
> [A light sprinkle] [A thick layer] [Melted over the top] [Not sure]

He taps *Not sure*. Three answered, three offered: **request 4**, the final analysis, follows at once.
Its `plate` works through a counter's two-ladle box, a cream-based sauce, the counter's usual
sprinkle of hard cheese, and a plain roll with nothing on it said. It returns:

| Item | Amount | Worth | Row | Detail (the reason) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pasta with mushroom cream sauce | 350 g | 220 kcal · P 6.3 · C 25.7 · F 10.3 per 100 g | 770 kcal | two ladles into a standard takeaway box; thick cream sauce from the answer | MEDIUM |
| Grated hard cheese | 10 g | 400 kcal · P 32 · C 0 · F 29 per 100 g | 40 kcal | a counter's usual sprinkle, since the amount was not known | LOW |
| Bread roll | 1 roll | 150 kcal · P 5 · C 28 · F 2 per roll | 150 kcal | small plain roll; no butter was mentioned | MEDIUM |

Note: *The cream in the sauce is the biggest unknown; a thinner sauce would be about 200 kcal less.*
Total **960 kcal** · P 30 · C 118 · F 41. **Four requests** in all.

The proposal screen opens on the three rows; he could change *350 g* to *300*, or tap *Use your
…* if he has a food called *Bread roll*. From Add something he taps **Log it**: three rows go on the
day, each an estimate, the first reading *350 g (two ladles into a standard takeaway box; thick cream
sauce from the answer)*. Had he come from My meals and tapped **Keep as a meal**, a named meal of the three
would be on My meals and nothing on the day.

Had he typed *"an apple"*, request 1 would have returned `needs_questions: false` with *Apple, 1
apple*, and the proposal screen would have opened as it does today: one request.
